### Complex driving factors

- We wanted to break this down into what we consider key drivers of these inequalities <!-- .element: class="fragment" data-fragment-index="1" -->
  - Age structure <!-- .element: class="fragment" data-fragment-index="2" -->
  - Underlying health conditions <!-- .element: class="fragment" data-fragment-index="2" -->
  - Vaccination coverage <!-- .element: class="fragment" data-fragment-index="2" -->
  - Social contact patterns <!-- .element: class="fragment" data-fragment-index="2" -->
- Each of these drivers interacts with another <!-- .element: class="fragment" data-fragment-index="3 -->

### People living in more deprived areas tend to: {: style="position: absolute; top: 80px; left: 60px; width: 80%;"}

- Have lower influenza vaccination uptake <!-- .element: class="fragment" data-fragment-index="1" -->
  - But eligibility based on health conditions and age <!-- .element: class="fragment" data-fragment-index="1" -->
- Have slightly fewer daily social contacts, more likely to be in same IMD quintile <!-- .element: class="fragment" data-fragment-index="2" -->
  - Again, depends on age <!-- .element: class="fragment" data-fragment-index="2" -->
