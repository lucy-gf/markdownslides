## *Why* is this division where it is?

### (According to this substack)

Initially, as the prevailing westerlies carried smoke from the City eastward, London’s wealthy merchants built upwind (in the west). <!-- .element: class="fragment" data-fragment-index="1" -->

Since then argued to be due to three policy decisions:<!-- .element: class="fragment" data-fragment-index="2" -->

- The 1947 green belt froze London’s spatial structure just as postwar migration was reshaping who needed housing and where. <!-- .element: class="fragment" data-fragment-index="3" -->
- Right to Buy, from 1980 onward, transferred council housing wealth to its beneficiaries, but the sold stock was never replaced. <!-- .element: class="fragment" data-fragment-index="4" -->

---

- Canary Wharf developments <!-- .element: class="fragment" data-fragment-index="1" -->
  - In 1981, the London Docklands Development Corporation stripped democratic planning oversight from a large section of the East End and handed it to private developers (on the promise that its gains would spill outward). <!-- .element: class="fragment" data-fragment-index="2" -->
  - The DLR and the Jubilee line extension were built for Canary Wharf. <!-- .element: class="fragment" data-fragment-index="2" -->
  - Tower Hamlets, which surrounds Canary Wharf on three sides, was left with some of the highest child poverty rates in the country. <!-- .element: class="fragment" data-fragment-index="2" -->
  - Little of the investment generated by the financial sector in Canary Wharf returns to local areas. <!-- .element: class="fragment" data-fragment-index="2" -->
